Understanding Hair Loss

Common causes and progression patterns

Genetic Factors

Androgenetic alopecia affects 50 million men and 30 million women in the US alone. Inherited traits determine sensitivity to DHT.

Prevalence 85% of cases

Hormonal Changes

Thyroid disorders, pregnancy, menopause, and PCOS can trigger temporary or permanent hair loss through hormonal imbalances.

Impact 60% treatable

Nutritional Deficiencies

Iron, zinc, vitamin D, and protein deficiencies are common culprits. Blood tests identify specific gaps to target.

Reversible 40% of cases

Non-Surgical Treatments

Proven therapies to slow, stop, and reverse hair loss

Minoxidil (Rogaine)

Topical solution that increases blood flow to follicles and extends the growth phase. Available in 2% and 5% strengths for men and women.

Men & Women

Finasteride (Propecia)

Oral medication that blocks DHT production, the hormone responsible for genetic hair loss. FDA-approved for men only.

Men Only

PRP Therapy

Platelet-Rich Plasma injections use your own growth factors to stimulate dormant follicles and promote thicker, healthier hair growth.

Men & Women

Low-Level Laser Therapy

Red light wavelengths (650-670nm) stimulate cellular activity in follicles, increasing ATP production and improving circulation.

Men & Women

Spironolactone

Oral anti-androgen medication for women that blocks hormonal effects on hair follicles. Effective for female pattern hair loss.

Women Only

Scalp Micropigmentation

Non-invasive cosmetic procedure that creates the appearance of thicker hair using specialized pigments matched to your hair color.

Men & Women

Frequently Asked Questions

Honest answers about PRP cycles, LLLT devices, combining medications, pricing, and contraindications

How often do I need PRP sessions, how much does a course cost, and how many sessions until I see real change
The standard PRP course at Barley is 4 initial sessions spaced exactly 4 weeks apart, followed by a single maintenance booster every 8-12 months. Each individual PRP session costs approximately $220 USD when paid as a 4-pack, or $280 if you pay per visit - the 4-pack saves about $240 total. Visible reduction in shedding usually happens after session 2, and vellus hair thickening into real growth is typically visible between session 3 and 4, so 3 months from start. If you have thicker donor miniaturization we sometimes recommend 6 sessions instead. All prices are written and all-inclusive, no separate centrifuge or injection fees.
What is the actual difference between the in-clinic LLLT laser device and buying a home-use LLLT helmet/cap myself
Three real differences. First is power density: our in-clinic LLLT panel delivers 5mW/cm2 uniformly across 100% of the scalp, while most consumer helmets deliver 1-2mW/cm2 and have blind spots at the vertex. Second is consistency: a 30-minute clinic session under medical staff supervision means full compliance every time, whereas home-use compliance drops to about 40% at 6 months according to our patient surveys. Third is protocol guidance: clinic LLLT is paired with your PRP injection schedule so the laser timing amplifies graft uptake. That said, home helmets absolutely work for maintenance if you are disciplined - we sell FDA-cleared models at clinic cost for patients who travel back home.
Can I use minoxidil / finasteride at the same time that I'm doing PRP injections, or do I need to space them
Yes, you can and you should - combination protocols consistently show 30-40% better density outcomes at 12 months vs any single therapy, which is why 82% of our non-surgical patients are on combined protocols. There is no interaction and no need to pause. Standard guidance is: minoxidil twice daily every day including injection days just don't apply it within 8 hours of a PRP shot, finasteride 1mg once daily with no exceptions, PRP injections on their scheduled 4-week cycle. The only pause we recommend is if you get scalp irritation from minoxidil the day before an injection, switch to the foam version or skip that single application.
What is the approximate total cost of a non-surgical plan, first year vs ongoing, compared to just doing a transplant
For a typical early NW2-NW3 patient, first-year non-surgical cost at Barley runs approximately $1,600-$2,400 USD all-in: $880 4-pack PRP + $300 one-time LLLT 12-session pack + $420 12 months of compounded finasteride + minoxidil foam + ketoconazole shampoo. Ongoing maintenance from year 2 is roughly $600-$850 per year. Compare that to a single FUE transplant of 2400 grafts at around $4,800-$6,200 one-time, plus meds still recommended long-term anyway. So non-surgical wins for 3-4 years of early-stage treatment, surgery becomes cheaper only when you know you definitely need grafts. Every patient gets a side-by-side table during the consultation.
If I start on the non-surgical plan but after 6 or 9 months I decide I want a transplant anyway, do any of the payments credit toward surgery
Yes, 100% of what you paid for the PRP 4-pack, the LLLT clinic sessions, and the diagnostic trichoscopy packages, up to a maximum of $1,500, can be credited directly toward the surgical balance if you book your transplant within 24 months of your first non-surgical visit. The only items that don't credit are monthly medication refills, individual maintenance boosters, and at-home devices because those were already consumed. Just mention the credit at booking and your patient file will be cross-referenced automatically - no receipt hunting required.
Is the monthly scalp care / hair care maintenance package actually worth adding for non-surgical patients, or is it upsell
It's worth it for roughly 6 out of 10 non-surgical patients, specifically anyone with seborrheic dermatitis, excess oil production, dandruff, or itchy/inflamed scalp. The monthly care package is $65 per visit and includes a detoxifying deep cleanse, salicylic acid exfoliation, anti-inflammatory LED blue light, and a custom nutrient mask depending on your scalp type that month. Patients who add it consistently show 18-22% less miniaturization at month 6 according to our internal data, because chronic scalp inflammation is a silent amplifier of DHT damage. If you have zero flakes and zero oil, skip it and just stick to meds + PRP.
What are the real contraindications - who should NOT do PRP, LLLT, minoxidil, or finasteride
Written absolute contraindications we enforce: PRP is not done if you are on anticoagulant medications, have platelet disorders, active hepatitis, uncontrolled diabetes, or any scalp infection at the injection site, and we defer 6 months post-pregnancy and while breastfeeding. Minoxidil is not for anyone with heart arrhythmia issues or severe scalp eczema - we patch test first. Finasteride / DHT blockers are never prescribed to women planning pregnancy, under 18 males without full hormonal workup, or patients reporting prior mood disturbances on 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors. LLLT has essentially no contraindications but we still avoid it the first 3 weeks post any scalp surgery. We review every medication you take at intake, not just the ones you remember to mention.
