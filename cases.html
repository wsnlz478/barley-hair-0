Frequently Asked Questions

Answers about our before-and-after case gallery

Are the cases in the gallery from actual patients of your clinic
Yes. Every photo in this gallery documents a real procedure performed at one of our Barley clinics. No stock images, no model photography, no images purchased from third-party libraries are used. Each patient featured has signed a photographic consent form specifically authorizing the use of their before-and-after images for educational and marketing purposes. We take patient privacy seriously and facial features are blurred or obscured whenever the patient requests it.
Can I request a video consultation with a patient who has a similar case to mine
We cannot arrange direct contact between prospective patients and past patients for privacy reasons. However, during your free consultation, your assigned surgeon can pull additional case files — including high-resolution photos, operative notes, and follow-up timelines — from our internal database that match your specific Norwood classification, hair texture, ethnicity, and aesthetic goals. Many of these cases are not published in the public gallery at the patient's request, so you will see a wider range during your consultation than what appears on this page.
If I have a similar starting point to a published case, can I expect the same result
The cases in the gallery serve as a realistic reference for what our techniques can achieve, but individual outcomes always vary. Your achievable density and hairline design depend on several personal factors including the quality and density of your donor zone, your individual hair shaft diameter, your age, and the total surface area requiring coverage. During consultation, your surgeon will show you multiple closely-matching cases and provide an honest assessment of how closely you can expect to replicate those results given your unique anatomy.
Have the case photos been digitally altered or edited
No. We have a strict no-retouching policy for our case photography. All before and after images are shot under standardized lighting conditions at fixed distances using calibrated cameras in our dedicated photo suite. The same patient is photographed from the exact same angles in the same chair for every follow-up visit. We do not apply skin smoothing, density painting, hair cloning, or any other digital modifications. The images you see on this page are the exact same images used internally to track a patient's surgical outcome.
Does the gallery include cases from patients of all ethnic backgrounds and hair types
Yes. Our patient base includes individuals from over 50 countries, and our gallery reflects that diversity. You will find cases featuring straight East Asian hair, wavy South Asian hair, curly African hair, coarse Middle Eastern hair, fine Scandinavian hair, and everything in between. Different hair textures require different implantation techniques and angle strategies — browsing cases that match your own ethnicity and curl pattern is one of the most useful things you can do before booking a consultation.
How recent are the cases shown in the gallery
Most of the published cases show procedures performed within the last three to four years, with at least 12 months of follow-up growth documented in the after photos. We intentionally replace older cases on a regular basis so the gallery always reflects the results achievable with our current generation of microneedle instruments and surgical protocols. If you would like to see results from patients treated 5, 10, or even 15 years ago to verify long-term permanence, we can pull archival case files during your consultation.
Do you track and publish negative or less-than-perfect outcomes
The public gallery naturally focuses on representative successful outcomes, as most patients who consent to photo sharing are happy with their results. That said, no clinic achieves a perfect result on every single case. During your consultation, we are completely transparent about complications and suboptimal outcomes — showing you corrective case examples, discussing what can go wrong, and explaining our complimentary revision policy for patients whose graft survival falls below our guaranteed threshold.
