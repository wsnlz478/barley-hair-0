What is FUT Hair Transplant?

FUT (Follicular Unit Transplantation), also known as the "strip method," is a traditional hair transplant technique where a strip of scalp is removed from the donor area, dissected into individual follicular units under a microscope, and then implanted into the recipient area. While FUE has become more popular, FUT remains an excellent option for patients requiring large sessions.

Understanding FUT

How FUT Works

A comprehensive overview of the FUT strip method

The Traditional Approach to Hair Restoration

FUT has been the gold standard in hair transplantation for decades. The procedure involves removing a narrow strip of hair-bearing scalp from the back of the head (donor area), which is then carefully dissected into individual follicular units under stereo microscopes.

These follicular units are then implanted into the recipient area using precision instruments. The donor area is closed with sutures, and the resulting linear scar is typically hidden by surrounding hair.

At Barley, we offer both FUE and FUT options, ensuring each patient receives the technique best suited to their specific needs and goals.

FUT Recovery Timeline

What to expect during your recovery from FUT hair transplant

Days 1-3

Rest at home. Mild swelling and discomfort are normal. Keep head elevated while sleeping. Take prescribed medications as directed.

Days 4-7

Swelling subsides. You can gently wash your hair. Small scabs form around grafts. Avoid strenuous activities.

Days 7-14

Scabs fall off naturally. Stitches are removed around day 10-14. Donor area healing progresses well.

Weeks 2-4

Transplanted hair begins to shed (shock loss) — this is completely normal. You can resume most normal activities.

Months 3-6

New hair growth begins. Noticeable improvement in coverage and density. Hair gradually thickens.

Months 9-12

Final results become visible. Hair has fully grown and matured. Enjoy your natural, permanent results!

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about FUT strip hair transplant

How long until the linear scar is no longer visible?

Immediately after suture removal (days 10-14), the scar appears as a raised pink line. By month 2-3 it flattens and fades to pale red. Most patients report that at month 4-6 the scar has softened to a thin, silvery-white line that is completely covered by even 1-2 cm of surrounding hair. Using trichophytic closure technique, the scar is further camouflaged by hair growing through and around it. In most cases, once the donor hair has regrown to a #2 buzz cut length or longer, you cannot locate the scar even when actively searching for it.

When are stitches removed, and is it painful?

Sutures (stitches) are typically removed between days 10 and 14 post-operatively. The removal itself takes about 10-15 minutes and is described by most patients as mildly uncomfortable rather than painful — more of a pulling or tickling sensation. No anesthesia is required. Some clinics use absorbable sutures that dissolve on their own, but non-absorbable sutures are preferred in FUT because they provide stronger, more even wound closure and slightly thinner scarring. You will be given a specific appointment date for suture removal during your discharge instructions.

Which Norwood / baldness grades benefit most from FUT?

FUT is particularly recommended for patients with Norwood Class 4, 5, 6, and 7 patterns. These grades involve extensive frontal, mid-scalp, and vertex loss that typically require 3,000+ grafts for meaningful coverage. In these cases, FUT's ability to harvest a large strip and produce 3,000-4,500 grafts in one session avoids the need for two or even three FUE procedures. Patients with earlier grades (NW 2-3) can absolutely do FUT as well, but FUE becomes equally viable because graft counts are lower.

Does FUT really produce higher density than FUE?

Yes, for two reasons. First, the strip is dissected under a stereo microscope by skilled technicians, which results in slightly lower follicular transection rates — meaning more of your grafts arrive at the recipient site with both hair follicles intact per follicular unit. Second, because the grafts are not individually punched out of the scalp, there is slightly less crush or thermal trauma to the root sheath. Clinically this translates into approximately 5-10% higher final density at the 12-month mark for large sessions, though for smaller sessions the difference is barely noticeable.

What is the widest donor strip that can be safely taken?

Most experienced surgeons will take a strip between 1.0 cm and 1.8 cm in width. The upper safe limit depends on scalp laxity — how loose and stretchy your skin is. Patients with good laxity can comfortably tolerate up to 2.0 cm, while tighter scalps may be limited to 1.0-1.2 cm to avoid excess closing tension. Wider strips produce more grafts but increase the risk of a wider, thicker scar and a sensation of tightness that lasts for months. An experienced surgeon will measure your laxity during the consultation and recommend a strip width balanced for yield and cosmetic outcome.

What happens if the closure has too much tension?

Excessive tension at the closure line is the single most common reason for a wider or thicker FUT scar. Tension pulls the skin edges apart slightly during healing, which stimulates the body to lay down extra scar tissue. In the immediate post-op period it also causes prolonged soreness, a "pulling" feeling when you turn your neck, and occasionally small areas where the suture line dehisces (opens slightly). Proper tension assessment, laxity planning, layered closure technique, and a reasonable strip width all but eliminate this risk. If you notice your scar is widening after month 3, scar revision or micropigmentation can later improve the appearance.

Can I do FUT again for a second repair session?

Yes, most patients can safely undergo a second FUT procedure, typically no earlier than 8-12 months after the first. During the second session, the surgeon excises the original linear scar as part of the new strip, so you still end up with one single scar instead of two. Graft yield from a second strip is usually similar to the first as long as there is remaining donor hair above and below the original line. Third FUT sessions are less common but also possible when scalp laxity permits. Your surgeon will evaluate scar quality and scalp mobility during a follow-up appointment before scheduling a second strip harvest.
