Our Eyebrow Transplant Technique

Where surgical precision meets artistic vision

1

Custom Brow Design

Before surgery begins, our surgeon draws your new eyebrow shape directly on your face using a surgical marker. We work closely with you to achieve the ideal shape, thickness, and arch that complements your unique facial features. Your input is essential — we refine the design together until you are completely satisfied with the planned result.

2

Single-Hair Graft Selection

Eyebrow restoration exclusively uses single-hair grafts for the most natural appearance. Our technicians carefully select the finest, most delicate follicles from your donor area, as these produce the softest, most natural-looking eyebrow hairs.

3

Ultra-Precise Placement

Using our finest microneedle instruments, each graft is placed at the exact angle and direction of natural eyebrow growth. The front portion of the brow grows upward and outward, the middle section lies flatter, and the tail angles downward. This multi-directional approach is what creates truly authentic-looking eyebrows.

4

Strategic Density Mapping

We create a natural gradient density — fuller in the body of the brow and gradually softer toward the front and tail. This graduated look is characteristic of natural eyebrows and avoids the artificial "solid block" appearance that less experienced clinics produce.

Eyebrow Transplant: Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to the questions our patients ask most often

Which donor area gives the best hair for eyebrow transplants — regular scalp, nape, or behind the ear
By far the best donor hair for eyebrows comes from the postauricular strip directly behind the ear, plus the lowest part of the occipital nape. The hair in these two zones has a much finer calibre — often half the thickness of regular crown-occipital hair — and a natural soft wave that lies perfectly flat against the brow ridge instead of sticking straight up. Regular thick scalp hair from the upper donor zone can be used in a pinch, but it tends to look coarse and brillo-pad like once it grows in. We almost always harvest 80% of brow grafts from behind the ear and 20% from the fine nape hairs.
How do you actually design the direction, angle, and fan shape so each hair points the right way
Eyebrow direction design is the single most important detail that separates a great result from a bad pluggy one. We break each brow into three zones. The inner head (closest to the nose): hairs grow almost straight up, about 70 to 85 degrees from the horizontal. The middle body: hairs sweep outward and slightly up at 30 to 50 degrees, fanning like the ribs of a palm leaf. The outer tail: hairs run nearly horizontal, 5 to 15 degrees, pointing down toward the temple. Every single graft is individually rotated with forceps before insertion so the cuticle exits the skin in exactly the right orientation. If even 10% of hairs point the wrong way, the brow will look spiky and unnatural.
Since the grafts come from scalp hair, will the new eyebrow hair be stiff and coarse like head hair instead of soft brow hair
If the surgeon harvests from the wrong donor area, yes — you absolutely can get that coarse hair-brush look, and it is the number one reason for eyebrow transplant revision cases. The way we avoid it is simple: we never use the thick coarse terminal hairs from the upper occiput. We harvest exclusively from the postauricular strip behind the ear plus the lowest nape hairs, both of which are naturally 30 to 50 percent finer in diameter. After six to nine months of growth on the brow, the hair continues to soften slightly as it adapts to the thinner facial skin environment. In practice, 95% of our patients say the texture matches their natural brow hair closely enough that nobody notices.
How soon after eyebrow transplant can I safely use brow pencil, brow powder, or get new microblading or tinting done
We split this into two timelines: makeup away from the graft line, and makeup directly on the brows. For eyeshadow, concealer, and foundation around the eye socket area — you can resume those after day 7 as long as you apply them very gently and do not rub the brow ridge. For actual brow pencil, powder, gel, or pomade placed directly on the graft sites, we recommend waiting a minimum of 30 days, and ideally 45 days, because the crusts and tiny open follicle wounds need to seal completely first. For semi-permanent services like tinting, microblading, nano-brows, or henna brows: minimum 6 months. The reason is that the implanted follicles are still anchoring down and any procedure that drags a needle or sharp blade along the skin can dislodge grafts that have not fully vascularised yet.
What percentage of eyebrow transplant patients are women, and roughly how many individual hairs are implanted for a typical female brow
At our clinic, women make up approximately 78 percent of eyebrow transplant patients. For a typical female brow that has medium sparseness but some existing hair — not completely bald — we usually place 180 to 260 single-hair grafts per side, so 360 to 520 total. For completely bare brows from birth, alopecia, or burn scarring, the range goes up to 250 to 320 per side. Every single graft is a single-hair FU — we never use 2-hair or 3-hair grafts in the eyebrow line, because even one multi-hair graft placed on the surface of the brow creates a visible dark clump that looks unnatural up close and in flash photography.
If my first eyebrow transplant is not dense enough after it fully grows in, can I add more density in a second session
Absolutely — density touch-ups are extremely common and usually very straightforward. The main reason patients want a second pass is simply that they love the first result so much they want it a bit fuller, not that anything went wrong. We usually recommend waiting a minimum of 10 to 12 months before any touch-up, because transplanted brow hairs continue to thicken and improve in calibre right up to the 12-month mark. A second session typically adds another 50 to 120 grafts per side, going into the tiny gaps between the existing transplanted hairs without damaging them. The second session survival rate is usually identical to the first.
What happens if the angle is slightly off and some hairs start growing inward toward the eye — do they actually poke and irritate the eyeball
True corneal irritation from a misplaced brow graft is rare, but mis-angled hairs can absolutely grow downward and tickle the eyelid or rest on the lash line. If this happens there are three simple fixes. Minor cases: you simply snip the end of that particular hair with small brow scissors every two weeks — it takes 10 seconds and is completely painless. Slightly more annoying cases: we can do a quick 10-second electrolysis pinprick in-clinic to permanently kill that one mis-angled follicle. The worst-case scenario of 20+ badly angled hairs would require a small touch-up session to re-angle the zone, but this is extremely uncommon in experienced hands and we have never had to do it in our last 412 consecutive eyebrow cases.
