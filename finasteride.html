What is Finasteride

Finasteride is a prescription medication classified as a 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor. Originally developed in the 1980s for treating enlarged prostate (BPH), it received FDA approval in 1997 specifically for male pattern hair loss under the brand name Propecia. The drug works by interrupting the biochemical pathway that produces DHT, a hormone that genetically susceptible hair follicles are highly sensitive to. When DHT levels drop, the miniaturisation cycle that progressively thins and shortens hair is interrupted, allowing follicles to recover and resume normal growth patterns. Over 30 years of peer-reviewed clinical research confirms that a daily 1 mg dose reduces serum DHT by roughly 68% and scalp DHT by approximately 60%, making it the most potent oral anti-hair-loss therapy currently available. More than 40 million men worldwide have used finasteride, establishing an extensive safety database that supports its long-term tolerability.

How Finasteride Works

Blocks DHT Production

Finasteride inhibits the Type II 5-alpha reductase enzyme, which is responsible for converting testosterone into dihydrotestosterone (DHT). By blocking this conversion, finasteride significantly reduces DHT levels in the blood and, critically, in the scalp tissues where hair follicles reside. Lower DHT levels mean less stimulation of the androgen receptors on genetically susceptible follicles, effectively halting the miniaturisation process that causes hair thinning and balding in men with androgenetic alopecia.

Stops Hair Loss Progression

The primary benefit of finasteride is its ability to stop or dramatically slow the progression of hair loss. Clinical trials show that approximately 86% of men taking finasteride experienced either a halt in hair loss progression or visible regrowth over a two-year period. By maintaining existing hair that would otherwise be lost to DHT, finasteride preserves baseline hair density and provides a stable foundation for additional treatments such as hair transplantation or topical minoxidil to build upon.

Promotes Hair Regrowth

While finasteride is most effective at preventing further hair loss, many patients also experience regrowth in areas where follicles have recently miniaturised but not yet permanently closed. This regrowth is most pronounced at the crown and mid-scalp, with visible improvements in hair count and thickness typically appearing after 6-12 months of consistent use. The regrowth potential is higher when treatment begins at an earlier stage of hair loss, before follicles undergo irreversible damage.

Usage & Safety Precautions

Finasteride is taken as a 1 mg tablet once daily, with or without food. Consistency is key—the medication must be taken every day without interruption to maintain its therapeutic effect. It is important to understand that finasteride is a long-term treatment; if you stop taking it, DHT levels return to pre-treatment levels within a few weeks, and any hair maintained or regrown through the medication will gradually be lost over 6-12 months. Finasteride is approved for use in men only and is not recommended for women, particularly those who are pregnant or may become pregnant, due to the risk of birth defects. Women should not handle crushed or broken finasteride tablets. Potential side effects, though uncommon (affecting approximately 2-4% of users), can include decreased libido, erectile dysfunction, and reduced ejaculate volume. These side effects typically resolve upon discontinuation of the medication. All patients should have a thorough consultation with a qualified physician and undergo appropriate screening before starting finasteride. At Barley Hospital, our specialists provide comprehensive evaluation and ongoing monitoring to ensure safe and effective treatment.

Expected Results: What to Expect

Results from finasteride treatment follow a predictable timeline. During the first 3 months, patients typically notice a reduction in daily hair shedding as DHT levels drop and follicles begin to stabilise. Between months 3-6, early signs of improvement may appear, with hair loss visibly slowing or stopping. By months 6-12, many patients observe measurable increases in hair density and thickness, particularly at the crown and mid-scalp regions. Maximum benefits are generally achieved after 12-24 months of continuous treatment. After the two-year mark, the medication continues to maintain results with ongoing use. It is important to document progress with standardised photographs, as gradual improvements can be difficult to notice day to day. Finasteride works best as part of a comprehensive hair restoration strategy—many of our patients use it in combination with minoxidil, PRP therapy, and/or hair transplantation for optimal, long-lasting results. Our medical team at Barley Hospital develops personalised treatment protocols and provides regular follow-up assessments to track your progress and adjust as needed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Expert answers to the most common questions patients ask before starting finasteride treatment

What exactly is DHT and why does it cause hair loss in the first place
DHT stands for dihydrotestosterone, a more potent form of testosterone created when the 5-alpha-reductase enzyme converts free testosterone in the body. For men with a genetic sensitivity to this hormone, DHT binds to androgen receptors on the follicles at the top and front of the scalp, progressively miniaturising them. Each growth cycle produces a thinner, shorter hair until eventually the follicle closes and stops producing hair entirely. Finasteride inhibits roughly 60% of scalp DHT, removing this ongoing assault so follicles can recover.
Why would a DHT blocker affect sexual function at all
DHT is one of several androgens that support sexual physiology, so blocking a significant portion of serum DHT can temporarily affect libido or erectile quality in a small subset of men. The clinical data consistently puts this at around two to four percent of users during the first three months. For most of those men, the effect is mild and resolves spontaneously as their body adapts. If it does not resolve, the changes are reversible within weeks of discontinuing the medication. Discussing your full medical history with your prescribing physician helps identify any individual risk factors beforehand.
If I do experience side effects, do they permanently go away after quitting
For the vast majority of men, yes. The 5-alpha-reductase enzyme resumes full activity within about two weeks of stopping the drug, so circulating DHT levels return to baseline and any drug-related sexual side effects dissipate. Anecdotal online reports of persistent symptoms are extremely rare relative to the millions of men who have used the medication worldwide, and large-scale epidemiological studies have not confirmed a distinct persistent syndrome. At Barley, we monitor patients closely and provide clear stop-and-restart protocols so every man knows exactly what to do at the first sign of concern.
Who absolutely should not take finasteride under any circumstances
Women who are pregnant or could become pregnant must never take finasteride, and should not even handle crushed or broken tablets, because DHT is essential for the normal development of male genitalia in a foetus and exposure can cause severe birth defects. Women who are breastfeeding should also avoid it entirely. Men with a known hypersensitivity to finasteride or any tablet excipients should not take it. If you currently have prostate cancer or are undergoing evaluation for elevated PSA, you must inform your urologist before starting. Everyone else should be screened with a proper history and blood work before prescription.
What is the difference between 1 mg finasteride and the 5 mg Proscar dose
They are the same active molecule but at completely different doses meant for different conditions. The 1 mg tablet, sold originally as Propecia, was specifically dosed and FDA-approved for male pattern hair loss. The 5 mg tablet (Proscar) is approved for benign prostatic hyperplasia because the prostate tissue is far more sensitive to DHT reduction than the scalp. Taking 5 mg daily for hair loss does not improve results beyond what 1 mg achieves, but it unnecessarily increases your total exposure and the odds of side effects. Stick with the 1 mg dose for hair restoration purposes.
Can women use finasteride for their own pattern hair loss
Not in the routine sense. The official label is for adult men only, and any woman of childbearing potential should absolutely avoid it because of teratogenic risk. Postmenopausal women with confirmed androgenetic alopecia are occasionally prescribed finasteride off-label by a dermatologist in carefully monitored cases, but results are mixed and the safety data is far thinner than it is for men. For female patients at Barley, we prefer female-safe protocols with minoxidil, nutritional therapy, PRP, and transplant options — we do not prescribe finasteride to women as a standard protocol.
How does finasteride affect my PSA blood test and prostate screening
Finasteride lowers serum PSA levels by roughly fifty percent after six months of consistent use. That means a normal PSA reading while on finasteride is actually more concerning than the same number off the drug, because the true unadjusted value would be roughly double. Any physician ordering your PSA must know you are taking finasteride so they can apply the standard correction factor when interpreting the result. Bring the medication list to every annual check-up and never withhold this information from your urologist or primary care doctor.
How is finasteride different from dutasteride, and why not just use dutasteride instead
Both drugs block DHT, but dutasteride hits both Type I and Type II isoforms of the 5-alpha-reductase enzyme while finasteride primarily blocks Type II. This means dutasteride can suppress serum DHT more deeply, often 90% or more, and it stays in the body for weeks rather than hours. However, dutasteride is only FDA-approved for enlarged prostate, not hair loss, and its much longer half-life means any side effect takes far longer to wash out if you stop the drug. For routine hair restoration, finasteride 1 mg remains the evidence-based first-line choice because the benefit-to-risk profile has been studied for over thirty years.
