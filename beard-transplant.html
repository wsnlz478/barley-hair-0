Because the transplanted hair is your own, it behaves exactly like natural facial hair. Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about beard restoration and facial hair transplant

Where on the body do you typically harvest the donor hair for a beard transplant
The scalp donor area at the back and sides of the head is always our primary and preferred source. The occipital scalp provides follicles with the ideal calibre and permanent growth characteristics. When scalp supply is limited — or when we need extra coarse follicles for dense jaw coverage — we can supplement with beard hair harvested from below the jawline (for patients who already have some) and, more commonly, chest hair. Chest hair is naturally thick and pigmented, which makes it excellent for building a dense-looking lower beard and jawline. All harvesting is done via 0.6mm microneedle FUE.
What graft density is appropriate for a beard transplant, and how many FU per cm² should I expect
Natural facial hair density varies by ethnicity but typically falls between 40 and 70 follicular units per square centimetre. In beard transplantation, we target approximately 35 to 55 FU/cm² — not 100% replication, because scalp-derived grafts have a slightly larger shaft and provide more visual coverage per unit. The mustache and central goatee area receive the highest density (around 50 FU/cm²), while the outer cheeks and upper neck are planted a little lighter (35 to 45 FU/cm²) to maintain a natural gradient.
Is it possible to do a truly full coverage, heavy-duty full-beard transplant including the cheeks and sideburn connection
Yes, full-face beard coverage from sideburn-to-sideburn and down to the neckline is absolutely achievable — but it requires a careful donor assessment and often a two-session plan. A complete heavy full beard on a medium-to-large male face typically takes 2,500 to 4,000 grafts. Patients with excellent scalp donor density and laxity can often complete this in a single long session. Patients with thinner donor supply will do the jaw, mustache, and chin in session one, then top up the cheeks and sideburns in session two approximately 8 to 10 months later.
How soon after a beard transplant can I safely shave with a razor without dislodging the grafts
We ask patients to avoid any direct blade shaving on the transplanted area for a minimum of 14 days. You can use an electric trimmer on its longest guard setting as early as day 7 if you need to tidy up native beard hair around the transplant zone, but do not touch the grafted sites directly. At day 15, most patients can begin using a regular razor with a light, single pass. Avoid pressing hard, going against the grain, or pulling the skin taut until approximately day 21. By week 4, you can shave exactly as you did before surgery.
What causes unnatural stubble direction in a bad beard transplant, and how do you avoid that risk
Unnatural or "pluggy" stubble almost always comes from three mistakes: planting perpendicular to the skin instead of following the natural 25 to 45 degree angle, ignoring the regional direction change across the face, and using grafts that are too large (3-4 hair FU) in the front edge. We prevent this by (1) mapping every direction change on the face with a pen before any anaesthetic, (2) using a custom angled implanter for every region — mustache pointing down, cheek angling slightly outward, chin pointing straight down, neck pointing upward toward the chin, and (3) reserving single-hair grafts exclusively for the perimeter outlines of your beard style.
If donor hair is taken from the chest or other body areas, does the harvesting leave any marks or harm the body long term
No, there is no long-term harm and the cosmetic donor scarring is essentially invisible when we use the 0.6mm microneedle FUE system. The 0.6mm punch creates micro-incisions that heal in roughly 3 to 5 days and leave dot scars smaller than the diameter of a hair shaft — completely undetectable under even 1mm of hair or stubble. Chest-hair harvesting leaves no visible marks because the surrounding native chest hair covers any faint dots. We also stagger harvest sites rather than clearing a single area, so your existing body hair distribution looks entirely natural after full healing.
Will the transplanted mustache and upper lip hair grow too long and require frequent trimming, or does it adapt to facial hair speed
Because the follicles originally came from your scalp, they do keep their scalp growth rate — which means they will grow faster and longer than native facial hair if left completely untrimmed. For most patients this is actually a benefit, because mustache and philtrum hair that grows slowly can be frustrating. In practice, you will simply trim your mustache during your normal beard-grooming routine, just as you would trim a naturally fast-growing mustache. The adaptation period is typically less than a month, and many patients comment that they prefer having slightly faster growth because their new mustache fills into shape sooner.
