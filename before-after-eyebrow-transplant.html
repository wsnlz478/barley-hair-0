FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Honest answers about what the comparison photos really show

Are the before-and-after comparison photos retouched or digitally edited?
No. We follow a strict no-retouching policy for all clinical eyebrow transplant photography. The only digital processing applied is standard white balance color correction to match the calibrated studio lighting between the two shooting dates. We never use frequency separation skin smoothing, digital hair painting, liquify warp, brow shape enhancement, cloning, or any form of Photoshop manipulation on the brow itself. Every case presented is auditable against the patient's unedited raw CR2 files stored on our clinical image server and available for your review in the consultation room upon request.
How do you guarantee identical lighting conditions between before and after shoots?
We shoot every eyebrow case in the same dedicated VECTRA 3D photo booth using fixed 5500 K daylight-balanced ring light geometry, standardized f/8 aperture, ISO 100, and identical patient head position locked by laser-aligned chin and ear rests. The booth itself is light-sealed so no ambient window light enters. We photograph a calibrated X-Rite ColorChecker passport in every single session so if any minor drift in the LED panel output occurs over time we can mathematically correct luminance and chrominance back to the exact baseline in post using standardized profiles.
Which result group in the gallery will I most likely match with my own case?
Your likely result tier depends on four input factors that we score during consultation: existing native brow density on a 1–5 scale, laxity of the forehead and glabellar skin, hair caliber match between occipital donor and your original brow hair, and whether you are a first-time patient or a revision patient. We assign each new consult a letter grade from A through D and show you only cases within the same grade cohort — group A are virgin high-density cases, group B are medium-density over-tweezed gaps, group C are partial alopecia and major gaps, group D are total alopecia, microblading failure revisions, and transgender gender-affirming brow reshaping.
Can an eyebrow transplant result be combined later with cosmetic tattooing or microblading?
Yes, they stack beautifully and many patients choose this hybrid approach, but the sequencing matters critically. The transplant must be fully matured first — we require a minimum 14-month gap after your final FUE session before any permanent cosmetic pigment work is performed over the same skin. If you tattoo too early the pigment will track along the neo-scarred implantation channels and blur, and the needle trauma can shock out young grafts still settling. Once fully healed the transplant hairs provide the 3D structure and tactile depth while the microblading fills in any remaining sparse gaps optically.
Roughly how many individual transplanted hairs does a natural eyebrow typically need?
A full bilateral adult eyebrow reconstruction normally requires 180–320 single-follicle units split between the two sides — so approximately 90–160 grafts per brow. The actual number depends on surface area measured from the calibrated VECTRA overlay, target brow style (soft natural tapered vs. bold structured block), and how much existing native hair the patient still has. A pure gap repair for a small 8 mm break may only need 40–70 grafts total while a complete bilateral alopecia totalis reconstruction with no native hairs can climb to 350–420 grafts for a dense, theatrical-quality result.
My new transplanted eyebrow hairs fell out around week 3 — is this normal shock loss?
Completely normal, and it is in fact expected rather than concerning. Telogen effluvium shock loss affects between 60% and 90% of eyebrow transplant follicles between day 14 and day 42 regardless of surgeon skill. The hair shaft itself sheds because the follicle was temporarily traumatized during extraction and reinsertion, but the dermal papilla stem cells deep in the skin remain intact and will begin regrowing a brand new anagen hair starting around month 3. You can tell true shedding from actual graft failure because shed hairs come out cleanly without scabbing, and the follicle pit closes within hours without redness.
Some of my implanted eyebrow hairs are growing downward and poking into my eye — how do I fix reverse or misdirected growth?
Misdirected hairs happen in roughly 4–7% of eyebrow transplants because even the best 0.6 mm implanter pen cannot achieve perfect angular precision for every single graft on curved, mobile brow skin. The correct first-line treatment is brow training with a clear disposable brow lamination glue applied nightly with a clean spoolie to gently lay the stubborn hair flat in the correct direction for 8–12 consecutive weeks. If that fails, we can perform a free mini revision under local at month 10 where the surgeon gently lifts each offending follicle using a 19-gauge micro-cannula and reorients it to the proper angle. We never do electrolysis on transplanted eyebrow hairs.
Will trimming, plucking stray hairs, or getting a professional brow shaping damage my transplanted follicles?
Normal trimming with small curved brow scissors and gentle shaping with a sharp stainless steel slant tweezer are both perfectly safe after month 5, once the follicle has anchored deep into the subcutaneous fat and the epidermis over the implantation site is fully mature. We do ask that you avoid aggressive thread brow threading for the first 9 months because that technique pulls at a 45-degree angle with enough force to avulse a shallowly anchored graft in the first trimester. For professional salon shaping we recommend providing a printed aftercare card to your esthetician so they understand which follicles are new and should be handled with extra caution during the first year.
