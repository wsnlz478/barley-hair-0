What Is Sideburns Transplant?

Restore facial symmetry with permanent, natural-looking sideburns

Transform Your Facial Contour

A sideburns transplant is a specialized FUE procedure that restores missing or thin sideburns by transplanting healthy follicular units from your donor area to the sideburn region. The result is permanent, natural-looking facial hair that grows just like your natural sideburns.

Whether you have patchy sideburns, thin growth, or genetics that prevent facial hair development, this procedure creates the full, even sideburns you've always wanted.

  • Permanent, natural-growing hair
  • Can be shaved, trimmed & styled
  • Restores facial symmetry & balance
  • Blends seamlessly with existing facial hair

What Sets Our Sideburns Transplants Apart

The reasons men from around the world choose Barley for their sideburns restoration

Shave, Trim & Style Just Like Natural Sideburns

Because the transplanted hair is your own, it behaves exactly like natural facial hair. Shave it clean, grow it out, trim it to your preferred length, or shape it with a comb — the hair grows continuously just like real sideburns and requires the same regular grooming. That's exactly what you want from an authentic result.

Mastery of Multi-Directional Growth

Facial hair growth is far more complex than scalp hair. Sideburn hair typically grows downward and slightly backward. Our surgeons replicate this directional pattern with precision, creating sideburns that grow exactly as nature intended — never looking obviously transplanted.

Thick, Masculine Hair Texture

We specifically select the thickest, most robust follicles from your donor area so your transplanted sideburns have the coarse, dense characteristics of natural facial hair. This careful graft selection is what gives your new sideburns their authentic masculine look and feel — even to the touch.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about sideburns restoration and facial hair transplant

Roughly how many grafts are typically needed for sideburns — is it per side or total
Graft numbers for sideburns are usually quoted per side, then doubled for bilateral work. A short, natural-looking standard width sideburn (ending level with the earlobe) typically takes 250 to 400 grafts per side, or 500 to 800 total. Extended sideburns that taper down to the jawline need 400 to 600 per side, or 800 to 1,200 total. If you want the long, connected-to-beard style that runs all the way to the mandible angle, budget for 550 to 750 per side. Before every procedure, we draw the exact shape on your face and count the planned recipient sites so you know exactly how many grafts you will receive.
Are sideburns actually hair on the skin surface, or is there something structurally different about the sideburn area
Sideburns are structurally normal terminal scalp hair, not facial beard hair. The sideburn area is part of the temporal scalp that simply extends down the face past the zygomatic arch — the skin, follicle depth, sebaceous glands, and hair texture are all scalp-like, not beard-like. This is excellent news for transplantation because it means the donor hair from your occipital scalp is a perfect texture match, not a "scalp hair on face" mismatch the way beard grafts can be. The only special requirement is the angle: sideburn hairs are not vertical, they point downward and slightly backward toward the ear, so placement direction matters a lot.
If I am connecting new sideburns to an existing beard, will there be a visible seam or dividing line
A visible seam is the most common complaint about poorly done sideburn-to-beard transitions, and it is entirely preventable with proper technique. We avoid a hard line by using a three-zone taper: the bottom 8mm of the sideburn uses single-hair grafts at reduced density, the next 8mm above the beard uses a mix of single and double hairs, and only the upper sideburn gets full standard density. We also feather the leading edge of the beard upward by a few millimetres so the two zones overlap visually. When done correctly, you cannot find the boundary even under close inspection.
I wear my hair very short — around a 3mm buzz. Will a sideburn transplant be visible or hidden at that length
At 3mm, most good sideburn transplants are invisible if the surgeon used 0.6mm or smaller punches and staggered the recipient sites. The two things that give away a sideburn transplant at very short length are (a) linear rows of plugs and (b) oversized punch scars on the donor. We use 0.6mm microneedle punches on the donor side and random-stagger recipient site placement instead of neat rows, so on a 3mm buzz the area looks like naturally dense temple-sideburn hair. We do recommend waiting until the 10-month mark before going to a grade-1 buzz, because early growth (months 3 to 8) can temporarily look sparser than the final density will be.
Sideburns are scalp hair, so do they grow too fast and require constant trimming compared to normal sideburns
The transplanted follicles do keep their original occipital-scalp growth rate, which is indeed slightly faster than the average genetically programmed sideburn growth. In practice this means trimming your sideburns perhaps once every 6 to 8 days instead of every 10 to 14 — a difference most patients never notice because it fits naturally into a normal haircut cycle. Many of our patients actually like the faster growth, because their new sideburns fill in and reach their target shape sooner after surgery than a slower-growing native sideburn would.
How does the graft survival percentage compare between sideburn transplants and regular scalp FUE on the crown or hairline
Sideburn survival is consistently equal to or slightly better than scalp-to-scalp FUE, for two reasons. First, the temporal and preauricular skin has an outstanding blood supply — it is thinner and more vascularised than the crown, so every graft gets excellent perfusion from day one. Second, sideburn grafts are almost always single- or double-hair follicular units, which handle handling and implantation better than the larger 3-4 hair grafts used on the scalp. In our practice, sideburn survival is routinely 93% to 96%, compared to 90% to 94% for crown work. If you follow the post-op instructions, survival is not something you need to worry about.
Is it possible to make really long sideburns that connect all the way down to the jawline or lower chin
Yes, extended jaw-length or even chin-length sideburns are absolutely achievable and we design them for patients quite often — especially musicians, actors, and people going for a more 70s-inspired or retro aesthetic. The two considerations are graft count and transition zone. Long sideburns tapering to a point near the mandible angle take roughly 550 to 750 grafts per side, and if you are also wearing a beard we blend the last 12mm of the sideburn into the cheek beard zone so there is no texture jump. Men with round or square faces often benefit from slightly longer, tapered sideburns because they visually lengthen the lower face.
