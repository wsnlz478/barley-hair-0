Male Hair Loss Guide

Understand your hair loss and find the right solution

You're Not Alone

Hair loss affects about 50% of men by age 50 and up to 80% by age 70. The good news is that with early intervention and the right treatment plan, you can effectively manage and even reverse hair loss. This comprehensive guide will help you understand what's happening, why it's happening, and most importantly, what you can do about it.

Types of Male Hair Loss

Identify what type of hair loss you're experiencing

Androgenetic Alopecia

  • Also known as Male Pattern Baldness
  • Most common type (95% of cases)
  • Receding hairline + crown balding
  • Genetic + hormonal cause
  • Progressive over time

Alopecia Areata

  • Patchy circular bald spots
  • Autoimmune condition
  • Sudden onset
  • Can affect any hair-bearing area
  • May regrow spontaneously

Telogen Effluvium

  • Generalized shedding
  • Stress-related (physical/emotional)
  • Usually temporary
  • Rapid hair thinning
  • Diffuse hair loss pattern

Causes of Male Hair Loss

Understand why you're losing hair

Genetics & Heredity

  • Family history is strongest factor
  • Inherited from either parent
  • Sets your pattern and timing
  • DHT sensitivity determined by genes
  • Can predict future loss pattern

Hormones & DHT

  • DHT = main culprit in hair loss
  • Causes miniaturization of follicles
  • Hair becomes thinner and shorter
  • Eventually follicles stop producing
  • DHT blockers can stop this process

Lifestyle Factors

  • Stress can accelerate hair loss
  • Poor nutrition weakens follicles
  • Lack of sleep impacts health
  • Smoking reduces blood flow
  • Excessive alcohol can worsen

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to the most common questions men have about hair loss, its causes, and available treatments

Do I really need treatment if I'm only Norwood Stage 1 or 2
Norwood Stage 1 is essentially a baseline mature hairline with no real miniaturization yet — most guys in their early 20s fall here and don't need treatment yet, just annual monitoring. Stage 2 is where the decision gets personal. If you have a strong family history of advanced baldness and you can see clear temple recession year over year, starting finasteride at Stage 2 can literally buy you a decade or more of full density. If there's no progression over 12 months, you can safely hold off. The biggest regret we hear from older patients is not starting at Stage 2 when they had the chance.
Is there enough donor hair for a Norwood Stage 7 patient
Norwood 7 is the most advanced pattern — only the horseshoe band at the back and sides remains. In most cases there IS enough donor hair for a strategic transplant, but the key word is strategic. A Norwood 7 patient cannot realistically cover the entire top of the scalp to teenage density. Instead, Barley surgeons focus on framing the face with a conservative, natural hairline and filling in the frontal third where baldness is most visible. Body hair from the chest or back can sometimes supplement a limited scalp donor supply. The goal is dignified coverage, not full restoration — managing expectations upfront is essential.
How many follicular units do men typically need by Norwood stage
Here is a realistic FU graft range Barley surgeons use for planning: Norwood 2 = 800-1,500 FU (temples only). Norwood 3 = 1,500-2,500 FU (temples + hairline). Norwood 3 Vertex = 2,000-3,000 FU (temples + crown). Norwood 4 = 2,500-3,500 FU (front + crown bridge). Norwood 5 = 3,000-4,000 FU (front-mid + crown). Norwood 6 = 3,500-4,800 FU (often split over 2 sessions). Norwood 7 = 3,000-4,000 FU (frontal focus only — strategic coverage). These are ballparks — donor density, hair caliber, and your goals all shift the final count.
How do I tell androgenetic alopecia apart from telogen effluvium
Male pattern baldness (AGA) is patterned and gradual — you lose hair specifically at the temples and crown in a predictable shape over years, and the hairs that come out are miniaturized (thinner, shorter, weaker caliber). Telogen effluvium is diffuse and sudden — you notice dramatically more shedding ALL over the scalp (sides and back included), usually starting 2-3 months after a trigger like surgery, crash diet, fever, or emotional shock. The shed hairs in TE are normal full-thickness hairs with white bulbs at the root. AGA never resolves on its own; TE almost always stops within 6-9 months once the trigger is gone.
What are the limits of no-shave FUE for men
No-shave (or long-hair) FUE lets men keep their existing hair length during extraction, which is great for privacy — but it has real constraints. First, graft count is typically capped at 1,500-2,000 FU per session because extraction takes longer through long hair. Second, your native hair needs to be at least 3-4 cm long to cover donor scabs. Third, men with very curly, coarse, or Afro-textured hair are usually not candidates because punch angulation becomes too difficult. Finally, no-shave works best for Stage 2-3 patients needing hairline work; Stage 5+ guys with large bald zones usually need a standard shaved procedure for adequate graft volume.
When do men actually start seeing real hair regrowth results
Timeline expectations are one of the most important things to get right. For medication alone: month 1-2 = nothing visible, maybe a temporary shed from minoxidil. Month 3-4 = first new baby hairs along the hairline. Month 6 = measurable density improvement on photos. Month 9-12 = final results you can judge. For transplant patients: weeks 2-6 = shock shed of transplanted hairs (normal). Month 3-4 = first sprouts poke through. Month 6 = about 50% of the final growth is in. Month 9 = 80% visible. Month 12 = full maturity. Everyone wants instant results; biology does not care. Be patient for 12 full months before judging any treatment.
For men — is minoxidil foam or liquid the better choice
Both the 5% liquid and 5% foam formulations of minoxidil are equally effective in clinical studies — the active drug is identical. The differences are user experience: liquid dries faster (3-5 min) and is slightly cheaper per ml, but it contains propylene glycol which causes scalp irritation, redness, or dandruff-like flaking in roughly 15-20% of men. Foam eliminates propylene glycol, so it's gentler and the #1 choice for guys with sensitive skin or scalp psoriasis. Foam is also easier to apply through existing hair without leaving a greasy residue. At Barley we recommend foam first for most patients; switch to liquid only if foam irritates you or you want the lowest possible cost.
